# Detailed Intuitive Guide to Conservation & Scattering Verification

This document provides a thorough, intuitive guide explaining every test, line of output, and plot panel generated by the conservation and scattering test suite (`test_conservation.py`). Use this as a reference to understand the physics, mathematics, and verification metrics.

---

## 1. The Physical Intuition (The Mental Picture)

Imagine you have a collection of **marbles (excitons)** moving through a crystal:
- Each marble has two properties:
  1. **Electronic State:** What floor of the building it occupies: **$S_2$** (upper floor) or **$S_1$** (lower floor).
  2. **Crystal Momentum $\mathbf{Q}$:** What direction/speed it has in the crystal lattice (discretized into $N_Q = 256$ momentum bins).
- This yields $2 \times 256 = 512$ total discrete "bins" where a marble can live in phase space.

The **Boltzmann collision operator ($K$)** is a giant $512 \times 512$ accounting ledger:
- When a marble at $(S_2, \mathbf{Q})$ bounces off a lattice vibration (phonon), it leaves its current bin and lands in another bin $(S_1, \mathbf{Q}')$ or $(S_2, \mathbf{Q}'')$.
- **Strict Conservation:** Every marble that leaves one bin **must** land in another bin. Unless light escapes (radiative decay $\gamma$), no marbles can appear out of nowhere or vanish into thin air.
- **Thermodynamic Asymmetry:** 
  - Dropping downhill from $S_2 \to S_1$ releases energy into a phonon (phonon **emission** $\propto 1 + n_{q\nu}$). This process can happen spontaneously even at zero temperature ($T = 0\text{ K}$).
  - Jumping uphill from $S_1 \to S_2$ requires absorbing an existing thermal phonon (phonon **absorption** $\propto n_{q\nu} = \frac{1}{e^{\hbar\omega / k_B T} - 1}$). This requires thermal energy and freezes out as $T \to 0\text{ K}$.


* **Explanation:** The simulation tracks two states. Band index `0` corresponds to $S_2$, and band index `1` corresponds to $S_1$.

```markdown

```
* **Explanation:** There are 256 discrete crystal momentum points in the Brillouin zone. Across 2 active states, the full phase-space matrix dimension is $(2 \times 256) \times (2 \times 256) = 512 \times 512$. Every row and column corresponds to a specific $(a, \mathbf{Q})$ pair.

```markdown
- Mean energies: E(S2) = 4.1207 eV, E(S1) = 4.1000 eV
- Energy ordering (high->low): S2 > S1
```
* **Explanation:** The test inspects the quantum chemistry energy payloads:
  - On average, excitons in $S_2$ possess $4.1207\text{ eV}$.
  - Excitons in $S_1$ possess $4.1000\text{ eV}$.
  - The mean gap is $\Delta \bar{E} \approx +20.7\text{ meV}$. This physically establishes that $S_2$ is energetically higher than $S_1$.

```markdown
- Radiative decay gamma: {'S2': 0.005, 'S1': 0.0} (applied at Q index 0)
```
* **Explanation:** Assume $S_2$ is the optically bright state. When an exciton is at $\mathbf{Q} = 0$ (the optical $\Gamma$-point where photon coupling occurs), it can radiatively decay to the ground state at rate $\gamma = 0.005\text{ fs}^{-1}$. $S_1$ is optically dark ($1\text{L}_b$, $\gamma = 0.0$), meaning it cannot emit light directly.

```markdown
- Rate capacities sum W: S1->S1: 54.709, S1->S2: 26.862, S2->S1: 32.363, S2->S2: 62.442
```
* **Explanation:** The total capacity of each scattering channel summed over all momentum pairs $(Q, q)$:
  - $S_1 \to S_1$ (intraband relaxation inside $S_1$): $54.71$
  - $S_2 \to S_2$ (intraband relaxation inside $S_2$): $62.44$
  - $S_2 \to S_1$ (**downhill** inter-state relaxation): $32.36$
  - $S_1 \to S_2$ (**uphill** back-scattering): $26.86$
  - Downhill capacity ($32.36$) exceeds uphill capacity ($26.86$), consistent with detailed balance and phonon statistics.

### The Test Results Table

| Test | Criterion | Value | Tolerance | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Column-sum conservation** | `max\|sum_i K_ij\| / max\|K\|` | `1.431e-15` | `1e-12` | **PASS** |

* **Physical Meaning:** 
  In the collision matrix $K_{ij}$, column $j$ specifies the fate of particles starting in bin $j$:
  - Diagonal entry $K_{jj}$: total loss rate out of bin $j$ (negative).
  - Off-diagonal entries $K_{ij}$ ($i \neq j$): gain rate entering bin $i$ from bin $j$ (positive).
  - Particle conservation requires the column sum to vanish identically: $\sum_i K_{ij} = 0$.
  - **Result:** The maximum relative column-sum residual across all 512 columns is $1.431 \times 10^{-15}$. This is at the fundamental limit of IEEE 754 64-bit floating-point precision ($\epsilon \approx 2.2 \times 10^{-16}$).

---

| Test | Criterion | Value | Tolerance | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **2. Dynamic conservation** | `max\|dN_tot/dt\| / N` | `1.930e-17` | `1e-12` | **PASS** |

* **Physical Meaning:**
  While Test 1 checks the raw matrix, Test 2 executes the actual code operator (`MultiBandTransitionMatrix.calc_scattering`) acting on continuous 4D wavepacket distributions in space and momentum.
  - It verifies whether $\frac{dN_{\text{tot}}}{dt} = \sum_{a, x, y, \mathbf{Q}} \left(\frac{\partial n}{\partial t}\right)_{\text{scat}} = 0$.
  - **Result:** The normalized fractional drift is $1.930 \times 10^{-17}$. Total exciton number is conserved under dynamic operation to 17 decimal places.

| Test | Criterion | Value | Tolerance | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **3. State-to-state flux balance** | `\|sum_a dN_a/dt\| / N (pure-state starts)` | `0.000e+00` | `1e-12` | **PASS** |

* **Physical Meaning:**
  This tests isolated state-to-state population transfer:
  - If wavepackets are initialized purely in $S_2$, particles scatter into $S_1$.
  - The rate of loss in $S_2$ ($\frac{dN_{S_2}}{dt} < 0$) must exactly balance the rate of gain in $S_1$ ($\frac{dN_{S_1}}{dt} > 0$).
  - **Result:** The net flux sum is `0.000e+00` (exact cancellation). Every exciton leaving $S_2$ arrives in $S_1$, and vice versa.

| Test | Criterion | Value | Tolerance | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **4. Emission > absorption capacity** | `E(S2)>E(S1) => sum W(S2->S1) > sum W(S1->S2) > 0` | `1.205e+00` | `1e+00` | **PASS** |

* **Physical Meaning:**
  Confirms the thermodynamic direction of the scattering rates:
  - Because $E(S_2) > E(S_1)$, $S_2 \to S_1$ transitions proceed via phonon emission ($\propto 1 + n_q$), while $S_1 \to S_2$ transitions require phonon absorption ($\propto n_q$).
  - The ratio $\frac{\sum W(S_2 \to S_1)}{\sum W(S_1 \to S_2)}$ must be strictly greater than $1.0$.
  - **Result:** The calculated ratio is $1.205 > 1.0$. Downhill relaxation is naturally favored over uphill thermal excitation.

| Test | Criterion | Value | Tolerance | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **5. Open-system decay law** | `\|dN/dt + sum_a gamma_a N_a(Q=gamma)\| / N` | `5.603e-18` | `1e-12` | **PASS** |

* **Physical Meaning:**
  When optical radiative decay is activated ($\gamma_{S_2} = 0.005$), particles can escape to the ground state.
  - The analytical loss rate must be:
    $$\frac{dN_{\text{tot}}}{dt} = -\sum_a \gamma_a N_a(\mathbf{Q} = \Gamma)$$
  - This test evaluates whether the discrete numerical operator matches the analytical decay rate.
  - **Result:** The residual between numerical execution and theory is $5.603 \times 10^{-18}$, confirming the decay sink is implemented correctly.

### **Panel (a): Column sums (conservation)**
* **What is plotted:**
  - **X-axis:** Column index from $0$ to $511$ across the entire phase space.
  - **Y-axis:** Absolute column sum $|\sum_i K_{ij}|$ on a logarithmic scale ($10^{-20}$ to $10^{-1}$).
  - **Dashed line:** Tolerance threshold ($10^{-12}$).
* **Visual observation:**
  - **Blue dots (Lossless Operator):** All 512 points lie between $10^{-18}$ and $10^{-15}$, well below the $10^{-12}$ tolerance line. Every single column sums to zero.
  - **Orange dots (Operator with Radiative Decay):** 511 points remain at $10^{-16}$, while **column index 0** jumps up to $5 \times 10^{-3} = 0.005$.
* **Takeaway:** The single outlier at index 0 corresponds to $(S_2, \mathbf{Q} = \Gamma)$. This confirms that radiative loss is restricted strictly to the optical $\Gamma$-point of $S_2$, while preserving all other 511 momentum channels.

---

### **Panel (b): Instantaneous inter-state flux**
* **What is plotted:**
  - **X-axis:** Normalized rate of population change $\frac{1}{N}\frac{dN}{dt}$ for each state ($S_2$ and $S_1$).
  - **Y-axis:** Instantaneous flux value.
* **Visual observation:**
  - **Blue bars (Pure $S_2$ Start):** $S_2$ loses population at $-0.127$, while $S_1$ gains population at $+0.127$.
  - **Orange bars (Pure $S_1$ Start):** $S_1$ loses population at $-0.106$, while $S_2$ gains population at $+0.106$.
* **Takeaway:** The bars are symmetric reflections across the zero line. The downward flux ($0.127$) exceeds the upward back-scattering flux ($0.106$), matching physical expectations.

---

### **Panel (c): Band energies and rate capacities**
* **What is plotted:**
  - **X-axis:** Exciton energy in electron-volts ($\text{eV}$).
  - **Y-axis:** Number of $\mathbf{Q}$-points at each energy (histogram).
* **Visual observation:**
  - **Orange histogram ($S_1$):** Centered around $4.1000\text{ eV}$.
  - **Blue histogram ($S_2$):** Centered around $4.1207\text{ eV}$.
  - An overlapping region exists between $4.105\text{ eV}$ and $4.115\text{ eV}$.
* **Takeaway:** The states are separated by an average gap of $\sim 20.7\text{ meV}$, which is comparable to thermal room temperature ($k_B T \approx 25.7\text{ meV}$). Because of this small gap and the energy overlap, excitons can absorb thermal phonons and back-scatter from $S_1 \to S_2$.

---

### **Panel (d): Open-system decay law**
* **What is plotted:**
  - **X-axis:** Comparison between numerical $\frac{dN_{\text{tot}}}{dt}$ and theoretical $-\sum_a \gamma_a N_a(\Gamma)$.
  - **Y-axis:** Decay rate for a randomized test state.
* **Visual observation:**
  - The two bars ("numerical $dN/dt$" and "-sum gamma N") are visually indistinguishable.
* **Takeaway:** The numerical scattering operator enforces the exact analytical open-system loss equation without unintended numerical damping.

## 4. Key Takeaways

1. **Exact Particle Conservation:** In the absence of decay, exciton number is conserved down to machine precision ($\sim 10^{-17}$).
2. **Proper Thermodynamic Directionality:** The rate tensors reflect phonon statistics—downhill relaxation ($S_2 \to S_1$) dominates over uphill thermal back-scattering ($S_1 \to S_2$), while permitting bidirectional population transfer.
3. **Selective Optical Decay:** Radiative decay operates exclusively at the designated state and momentum point ($S_2, \mathbf{Q} = \Gamma$) without disrupting phase-space conservation elsewhere.
